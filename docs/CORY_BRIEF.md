# Before you put the glasses on

The glasses see what you see. A small model notes what is in each frame: a cup, a screen, a person. A few times a minute a more careful model reads those notes, writes down what it noticed, and decides whether to speak.

It speaks only when a thoughtful person in the room would have. Then it talks like a coach who knows the research: what it saw, what that does to you, one specific thing to do. Most of what it notices goes to the log on the phone, not your ear. Three or four spoken lines in fifteen minutes is normal. Silence means it is watching, not broken.

What it cannot do yet:

- You cannot talk to it. It listens briefly, only after asking you a question; yes, no or a short answer works.
- It knows nothing it cannot see, so no sleep, steps or heart rate today.
- It does not know you yet. A few questions on the phone at the start (bedtime, wake time, what you care about) are all it has.
- It says each thing once.

Things to try:

- Get a coffee, tea or energy drink.
- Have a snack or something sweet, or sit down to eat.
- Pick up a supplement bottle or packaged food and read the label.
- Work at the laptop, phone beside it, for a while, then get up.
- Step to a window or outside.
- Have someone join you and talk.
- A glass of wine or beer, if it is evening.
- Drink some water.

Open "Glasses view" in the phone app to see what it sees. At the end the phone app shows the session: a summary, the log of what it noticed, and how it read the day.

Frames live in memory for ninety seconds and are discarded; only the handful behind a moment it reacted to are kept, and you can see them in the log.

Look not at how much it says, but whether the one or two things it does say are things only something watching you could have said.
