Longer life and a better day are the same project. Every bad decision costs twice: years later, and tomorrow morning. The glasses see the decisions no wrist device can: the 4 pm coffee, the second drink, the skipped meal, the screen at midnight, the injection taken or not. Each has a known effect with a known decay, and they compound across days. So the app scores two ceilings, not one score: Cognition, the percent of your cognitive ceiling you operated at today, and Body, the same for physical capacity. Bryan's number is aging 0.7 years per year; ours is how much of your ceiling you got to use today, this week, this month. The protocol is the set of windows that keeps you near 100 percent. The calendar shows the protocol laid over what actually happened: green where you were inside a window, red where you were not, with the real consequence written next to it. The system's job is to remove the decision, whisper once when it cannot, and show you what it cost or earned. A new mother with a crying baby operates far under her ceiling every day; year over year that is a healthspan problem even if she lives long. This app is for her as much as for Bryan.
